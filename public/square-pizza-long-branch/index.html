Grandma vs. Sicilian: what's the difference?

Both are square, pan-baked pies, but they eat very differently. A Grandma pie is the thin one: the dough is pressed into an olive-oiled pan and baked crisp, so you get a low, crunchy crust with the sauce and cheese right up top. A Sicilian is the thick one: the dough gets time to rise in the pan, so the crumb comes out taller and airier with a golden, fried-crisp bottom.

If you want a square that eats like a crisp flatbread, order the Grandma. If you want a square with real chew and height, order the Sicilian. Plenty of tables settle it by ordering one of each.

Good to know

What is a Grandma pie?

A Grandma pie is a thin square pizza baked in an olive-oiled pan, so the crust comes out low and crisp rather than tall and airy. Gigi's bakes them fresh at 140 Brighton Avenue in Long Branch — order online at gigislongbranch.com or call (732) 377-2468.

What's the difference between Grandma and Sicilian pizza?

Thickness. The Grandma is thin and crisp because the dough goes into the pan without a long rise; the Sicilian rises in the pan, so it bakes up thick and airy with a crisp golden bottom. Gigi's makes both, plus the vodka-sauce Drunken Grandma and the Sicilian Pan Pie.
